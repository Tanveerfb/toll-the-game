Ruling #107 · battle screen

Battle at 390

Three renderings of the same fight on a 390×844 phone, at true size — this is 798px tall, which is 844 minus the nav row the battle screen keeps.

Decided and built, 2026-08-21. This file is now the record of that decision rather than a set of questions: frame B is what shipped, with the card floor at 56px and merge arming from the card's own button. Each call below carries what was chosen and why. Ruling #118 in docs/HANDOFF.md is the authority; if the two ever disagree, the ledger wins and this file is stale.

Call 1 — where the six controls live  ·  B, the sheet

Tanveer: "i want the options to hide in a modal behind a button click." The rail was the right answer on a desktop, where 56px off the side of a 1440px window costs nothing. At 390 it was 14% of the screen, permanently, taken from the one region that has real work to do, and sitting under the top half where a thumb doesn't reach.

BuiltSkip and Speed stay in the open — Skip exists for the few seconds an animation is playing, and neither survives being two taps away. Log, Foe, Team and Exit sit behind Controls, which opens a bottom-anchored sheet: everything in it is something a thumb has to reach, so a centred modal would have been the wrong shape.
AlsoRailButton became ControlButton. A component named after a thing that no longer exists is the same failure as a skill outliving its subject.
Not takenThe dock (frame A). Five permanent buttons still hold ~56px of height, and the sheet gives that back for one extra tap on the four controls you touch least.

Call 2 — may a hand card shrink?  ·  B, floor at 56px

The hand holds up to eight cards (4 field units → 8). They were flex-1 with no minimum inside a container that already scrolled — so instead of scrolling, eight cards divided 390px into 43px slivers, and the container never scrolled at all, because nothing ever overflowed it.

Builtmin-w-14 — 56px. Six cards visible, swipe for the rest, and the row finally does what it was always built to do.
CaughtCentring had to change with it. Plain justify-center makes the first card unreachable once content overflows — a real bug, and one that only appears now that overflow actually happens. Auto margins on the end children centre while it fits and don't clip when it doesn't.

Call 3 — merge, on a phone  ·  the card's own button arms it

Merging was drag one card onto another, and a drag is the one gesture that can't be spared here — it fights the horizontal scroll of the row it happens in, on a card 56px wide.

BuiltTap Merge on a card → partners light, everything else recedes → tap one to commit. With a single partner it commits on the spot: there is nothing to choose, and the store would have picked the same card anyway.
WhyThe second tap earns its place only when there are two partners — and then it matters, because a merge grants +1 ult gauge to the eaten card's owner. That was a choice the code used to make for you.
KeptDrag still works, and is still the faster desktop path.

Call 4 — what a tile shows  ·  keep focus-fire, strip becomes a readout

The footer carried a 16px effect strip and a 20px focus-fire toggle alongside the name, HP number, HP bar and ult gauge.

BuiltThe strip is a readout. It was a button nested inside the tile — which is itself a button, to the same detail panel. Two targets, one of them unhittable, for one destination.
BuiltFocus-fire moved onto the portrait, top-right, at a real 44px. It's a per-turn combat decision and it was the smallest control on the screen. The "Ult Ready" banner pads around it.
NoteTap-and-hold on a tile opens its details — which already worked, because a long press ending in a release still fires a click. No code was needed for it.

Cut — the event ticker

Tanveer: "if someone needs to know what happened then they can just check the log." One line restating the last action, holding a 44px band in the tightest vertical space on the screen, duplicating what the log drawer holds in full. Its slot is where the control bar sits now.

His find — a skill you couldn't read

Not in the audit, and worse than it sounds: "on mobile, there is usually no way to see what skills or ults do when they are in deck." The card preview was hover-only, so on a phone there was no way to read a skill in battle at all — you played cards from memory.

BuiltTap plays a card; press-and-hold opens its full description — name, type, rank, power, the text resolved to that card's rank, and a footnote per mechanic it names. Shared with the desktop preview (CardDetail) so the two can't drift into telling different stories.
Then"it has to play a 3 sec commitment type progression circle that fills before the modal opens." A ring appears once the press outlasts a tap and fills over the remaining time; the modal opens when it completes. One constant (HOLD_DETAIL_MS) drives both the timer and the ring's CSS duration, so the animation can't promise a different length than the code enforces.
TunedBuilt at 3000ms, flagged as confirmation length — the pacing of "delete this permanently", not of "what does this card do" — and cut to 1500ms within the hour: "3s sure is long. maybe try 1.5sec?" The ring only has to prove the press was deliberate.
The catchOne gesture, three endings — and the middle one is the dangerous one. A quick release plays the card; a completed hold opens details; an abandoned hold does nothing. Falling through to "play the card" there would spend an action at the exact moment the player decided against something.
Knock-onHold used to light merge partners. Freeing it is what made the arming flow in Call 3 possible — the two changes are one decision, not two. And the row went touch-none → touch-pan-x: blocking every touch gesture was harmless while nothing overflowed and a trap the moment cards got a floor. Trade: no drag-to-reorder by touch, since that swipe now scrolls.

The rest of the mobile pass shipped alongside this: the button scale carries a 44px floor at every size, mechanic keywords open on tap instead of hover, the modal shells measure in dvh, the nav's counters and route links are 44px, and the arena's coach, detail panel and action slots are all sized.
